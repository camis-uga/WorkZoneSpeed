GDOT Research Project RP 25-13 · University of Georgia

Evaluating Speed Reduction Techniques in Construction Zones to Improve Safety

An evidence-based look at which speed reduction methods actually slow drivers down in Georgia work zones, so that workers and the traveling public get home safely.

Sponsored by GDOT & FHWA 2025 – 2028 Field radar + video data
Overview

Why work zone speeds matter

Work zones narrow lanes, shift traffic and put people a few feet from moving vehicles. Speeding in these conditions raises both the chance and the severity of crashes, especially rear-end collisions near queues.

This project investigates how well traditional and emerging speed reduction techniques work in Georgia construction zones. We combine a national literature review and a survey of State DOTs with field data collected at active GDOT work zones, and turn the results into recommendations and a Work Zone Safety Guide for GDOT.

What we're evaluating

Speed reduction techniques

Typical reductions below are drawn from published studies reviewed in Task 1.

Traditional

Dynamic speed feedback signs

Display each driver's speed back to them. Reported reductions of about 3–6 mph.

Traditional

Portable changeable message signs

About 1–4 mph for general messages, up to 7 mph when messages are vehicle-activated.

Traditional

Temporary transverse rumble strips

Alert drivers through sound and vibration, around 1–2 mph, and especially effective for trucks.

Traditional

Law enforcement presence

Ranked among the most effective methods by State DOT respondents.

Emerging

Queue warning systems

Sensors detect slowing traffic and warn approaching drivers on upstream message signs.

Emerging

Connected vehicle (V2X) alerts

Roadside units share work zone and hazard information directly with vehicles.

Approach

From roadside sensing to recommendations

Portable roadside radar and cameras are set up before, within and after each work zone to measure how drivers respond to the traffic control in place.

Sense

Traffic radar measures vehicle speeds while cameras record the scene for volume counts and context.

Align

Radar tracks and video are time-synchronized and matched vehicle by vehicle.

Measure

Speed distributions, compliance, flow and congestion state at each position through the zone.

Recommend

Compare techniques and site conditions to identify what works and where.
